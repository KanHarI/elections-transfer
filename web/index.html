How this is estimated

Votes are secret, so nobody knows how any individual moved. What is known is each ballot box's result in both elections. Boxes are grouped into clusters that cover the same voters in 2022 and 2026: polling places (the CEC's “ריכוז”) matched by their id, then by shared box numbers; places that can't be matched are pooled per locality, or per regional committee. Double-envelope ballots (soldiers, diplomats, hospitals, prisons) form one cluster of their own.

Each cluster is a closed population. On the 2022 side everyone either voted for a list, cast an invalid ballot, didn't vote, or wasn't yet on the roll (turned 18 or immigrated since). On the 2026 side the same people voted, didn't vote, or left the roll (mostly deceased). Lists under 0.2% of the national vote are grouped as “Other”.

    Does it recover known flows?

    We planted known flows in synthetic data with the same clusters and sizes, aggregated them to box level, and asked each model to recover them. Lower is better: the share of a 2022 list's voters sent to the wrong 2026 destination, averaged over lists.

    Caveats

    • This is ecological inference: aggregate results can't pin down individual moves, so every number here rests on the model's assumptions. Treat flows under a few thousand people as noise.
    • People who moved home between elections appear as “left the roll” in one cluster and “new voters” in another.
    • Double-envelope voters are a different population in each election (most 2022 soldiers have been discharged). They also stay on their home box's roll, so their numbers are removed from home-box abstainers, in proportion to registered voters.
    • New voters vs. leavers is split with a national death rate (3.1% over four years); only their difference is observed.
